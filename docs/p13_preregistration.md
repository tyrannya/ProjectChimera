## 1. The question

> Can a mechanically defined delta-hedged BTC spot/perpetual carry position —
> **LONG Binance spot BTCUSDT** against **SHORT Binance USD-M BTCUSDT
> perpetual** — earn robust positive net returns from funding and/or basis after
> **both** legs' realistic trading frictions, hedge maintenance and adverse basis
> movement, using only information available at each decision instant?

This is a **structural, non-directional** hypothesis. The position is built to be
indifferent to the next price move; what it is exposed to is funding and basis.

**It is not P4 again.** P4 read funding, open interest and basis as *predictive
information* for a directional model, and was screened out at Stage 1. P13 uses
funding and basis as the *payoff mechanism itself*. Those are different
hypotheses: a negative P4 says nothing about this one, and a positive P13 would
say nothing about P4.

**Nothing is fitted.** There is no train/test split, no hyperparameter, no model
family, and no threshold selected from data. The strategy is a fixed arithmetic
rule, which is why the design can be frozen this completely before it runs.

## 2. Evidence ceiling — read this before any number

**`EXPLORATORY / ADAPTIVE HISTORICAL STRUCTURAL FEASIBILITY EVIDENCE`.**

Historical Binance funding and basis were already public knowledge in 2026, when
this was designed. The designer was **not** blind to the era. So this screen can
*falsify* the structural mechanism convincingly, and it cannot *confirm* a future
edge. Genuinely stronger confirmation needs a strategy frozen before wall-clock
data that has not yet occurred, followed by sustained prospective paper
observation. A positive result does not lift this ceiling.

Two further honesty points, both recorded in the module rather than left to the
reader to notice:

- **One hindsight-informed parameter survives the design.** The always-on rule
  removes every funding-derived entry, exit and sizing parameter — but the **leg
  direction** remains. A SHORT perpetual receives when funding is positive, so
  choosing LONG spot / SHORT perpetual rather than the reverse fixes the *sign*
  of the entire funding payoff, and perpetual funding on major crypto pairs has
  been predominantly positive over the era being screened. The mechanism argument
  is real — retail leverage demand is structurally long — but the choice was not
  made in ignorance. Funding received and funding paid are therefore reported as
  two separate magnitudes per block, never netted, so a reader can see how much
  of any positive result came from the direction being right about the era.
- **The margin model was chosen with knowledge of a price fact.** See §8.

## 3. Sources

Four Binance archives, one venue, both legs. Same-venue by construction: a
cross-exchange carry would add basis and settlement risk this first screen is not
trying to measure, and it is a separate checkpoint.

| field | archive | role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| spot price | `data/spot/monthly/klines/BTCUSDT/1h/` | LONG leg fill; basis denominator |
| perpetual price | `data/futures/um/monthly/klines/BTCUSDT/1h/` | SHORT leg fill; basis numerator |
| funding settlement | `data/futures/um/monthly/fundingRate/BTCUSDT/` | the carry payoff |
| mark price | `data/futures/um/monthly/markPriceKlines/BTCUSDT/1h/` | funding notional base |

**Established from first-party evidence, not inherited.** The path grammar,
object and checksum naming, checksum algorithm and archive column layouts are
read from Binance's own published repository `binance/binance-public-data` at
commit `5c7f3197591c0d54d85dc43066226bc4c671d47a` — `python/utility.py::get_path`
for the paths, `python/enums.py` for the base URL, the `download-*.py` scripts
for the object names. That repository also settles a question this design could
not otherwise answer: `markPriceKlines` is a real published archive family, since
Binance ships an official downloader for it.

Two things that repository makes clear, both of which corrected an assumption
inherited from P4:

- **Archives can be revised.** Binance's README states that "archived files may
  be updated at a later date" and publishes an exhaustive changelog — including a
  **2022-08-08 kline update**, which falls inside this checkpoint's span. A
  closed candle and a realised settlement are final *as market facts*; the
  archive *object* carrying them is not guaranteed immutable. The acquisition
  therefore records both the published `.CHECKSUM` digest and an independently
  recomputed sha256 of the bytes actually received, so the frozen manifest pins
  what the result was computed from.
- **Spot timestamps change unit inside the span.** Binance's README: spot data
  from 2025-01-01 onward is in **microseconds**; before that, milliseconds. The
  futures archives carry no such note, so spot and perpetual sources may use
  *different* units for the same month. Units are resolved per object by
  `nn.trade_aggregates.resolve_epoch_unit` — which requires the parsed instants
  to land inside the archive's own calendar period under exactly one unit — and
  the resolved unit is recorded in the manifest. An archive whose unit cannot be
  resolved unambiguously is a refusal, not a guess.

The one source not covered by Binance's published code is the funding archive:
`binance-public-data` ships no `download-futures-fundingRate.py`. Its path rests
on `nn.p4_preregistration.FUNDING_ARCHIVE_INCEPTION_POLICY`, which records real
HTTP checks returning 200 for `2020-01`. That is stated plainly rather than
glossed.

REST endpoints are **not** a fallback for history: a row from REST is not a row
of the preregistered historical source.

**The committed spot snapshot is pre-Styx, not pre-boundary.** The filename says
so and it means it: `btc_usdt_1h_gen1_raw_pre_styx.parquet` spans
`2020-01-01T00:00:00+00:00 .. 2025-08-27T22:00:00+00:00` over 49,551 rows, so it
**contains roughly 2,415 rows at or after the P13 boundary** — the retired
P4-HOLD region. It must never be described as a pre-boundary file. Using it
therefore carries one explicit carve-out: a **truncating read at load**, before
any P13 computation touches a row, with the loader asserting the maximum
surviving instant is strictly below the boundary and recording it in the evidence
manifest. This is the only place the "a row at or after the boundary is a
refusal, not a filter" rule is relaxed; it is relaxed only for a file whose extra
rows were committed long before P13 existed, and a freshly acquired archive is
still refused rather than truncated. §4 carries the single full statement of this
carve-out, and the preregistration module is authoritative over both.

**The mark-price fallback is per archive object**, not all-or-nothing: any month
whose `markPriceKlines` object is unpublished falls back to the spot close for
that month alone, with the substitution flag reported per block and as a count of
affected settlements. Partial availability is the likely case, and an
all-or-nothing rule would have had no defined behaviour for it.

## 4. Data boundary

- **Span:** `2020-01-01T00:00:00+00:00` inclusive → `2025-05-19T08:00:00+00:00`
  exclusive.
- **Start** is where the sources begin, not a choice: Binance's own
  `PERIOD_START_DATE` is `2020-01-01`, P4 amendments A2 and A3 measured both the
  funding and perpetual-kline archives to begin at `2020-01`, and the committed
  spot snapshot starts at the same instant.
- **End** is the first instant of the retired **P4-HOLD** region. It is stricter
  than the Styx seal (`2025-08-27T23:00:00+00:00`), so it binds.
- **P4-HOLD is not read.** Retired, checkpoint null, not a spare holdout.
- **Styx is not read.** Sealed.
- **No manufactured holdout.** Splitting history that was already public in 2026
  does not create independence.
- Enforcement is fail-closed: a row at or after the boundary is a refusal, and
  the frozen evidence records the maximum observed instant so an auditor can
  check it without trusting the code. `nn/p13_carry.py::evaluate_block` asserts
  it too, on quotes and on funding settlements alike, so the sentence is true of
  the evaluator and not only of the acquisition.
- **One carve-out, and this is its single statement.** The boundary falls
  mid-month, so the final archive month (`2025-05`) necessarily contains rows on
  both sides of it. The rule is about research *observations*, not bytes on disk:
  that month is fetched whole — a partial object cannot be checksum-verified
  against Binance's published digest — and **truncated at load** before any P13
  computation touches a row, with the surviving maximum instant and the dropped
  row count recorded, and the manifest carrying the digest of the *whole*
  published object. The carve-out covers exactly two things: that one month, and
  the pre-existing committed spot snapshot of §3. Every other archive month lies
  wholly before the boundary and is refused rather than truncated.
  `DATA_BOUNDARY["the_boundary_straddling_month"]` in
  [`nn/p13_preregistration.py`](../nn/p13_preregistration.py) is authoritative.

## 5. Funding causality

The load-bearing rule: **a funding settlement is knowable only at its settlement
instant.** No decision at time `t` may use a settlement whose instant exceeds
`t`. The continuously published *predicted* rate is **never read** — it is a
forecast, it is revised, and reading it would put a later number into an earlier
row.

The strategy takes **no funding signal at all**. It is always-on within a block,
so no entry, exit or sizing decision is derived from any funding observation.
That removes the entire class of funding lookahead by construction rather than by
checking for it. Funding enters only as a cash flow, at its own instant, while a
position is already open.

**Boundary tie rule**, explicit because it fires in every block — each block
opens at 00:00 UTC on 1 January, which is itself a settlement instant:

> apply settlements with `open_instant < settlement_instant <= close_instant`

A settlement *at* the open is not applied (the position did not hold through its
accrual window); one *at* the close or liquidation instant is.

**Execution price.** Both kline sources are stamped by candle **open**, so at
grid instant `t` the only price in that row knowable at `t` is the open. Fills —
entry, exit, and any liquidation-forced close, on both legs — use the **open**.
The close is used only for marking (basis, mark-to-market, equity, funding
notional) and is never a fill price. Leaving this unstated would have been a
one-hour lookahead at both ends, which is 100% of the price PnL.

**Required positive controls.** The suite must *fail* when funding is shifted one
settlement into the future, when a settlement is made visible before its instant,
and when a settlement is applied twice.

## 6. Position and capital contract

```text
LONG  Q BTC spot          (Binance spot BTCUSDT)
SHORT Q BTC perpetual     (Binance USD-M BTCUSDT)
```

Equal quantity is what makes the position delta-neutral: net BTC exposure is
identically zero, not approximately zero.

| item | value |
| --- | --- |
| total starting capital | 1,000,000 USDT |
| spot allocation | 500,000 USDT |
| perpetual allocation | 500,000 USDT |
| hedge ratio | 1.0 BTC short per 1.0 BTC long |
| leverage | exactly 1x gross, never more |
| margin mode | portfolio (cross) collateral across the two legs |
| rebalances | none |
| return denominator | total committed capital |

**Why a real scale rather than 1.0.** A denominator of 1.0 USDT is
arithmetically incompatible with the venue filters this design invokes: Binance's
BTCUSDT perpetual has `step_size` 0.001 BTC and `min_notional` 100 USDT, so at a
2020 price near 7,195 USDT half of 1.0 USDT buys ~0.00007 BTC, which floors to
**zero**. The design would have opened no block and returned INVALID for a units
reason. Returns are quoted as *fractions* of the declared capital, so no gate
threshold moves.

**Sizing.** `Q` is the largest equal quantity **both** allocations can fund:

```text
Q_spot_bound = spot_allocation / (spot_price x (1 + spot_fee + spot_slippage))
Q_perp_bound = perp_allocation / (perp_price x (1 + perp_fee + perp_slippage))
Q            = step_floor(min(Q_spot_bound, Q_perp_bound))
```

The minimum over both legs, not the spot leg alone. Sizing from spot only would
make the perpetual's cash requirement exceed its allocation as soon as the
perpetual trades ~5 bps above spot — that is, in exactly the contango regimes a
carry position exists to harvest — so the design would have refused to open in a
way correlated with the phenomenon under study. `step_floor` uses the **coarser**
of the two instruments' step sizes so one `Q` is legal on both.

**No rebalancing, and this is a property rather than an omission.** The hedge is
quantity-matched, so net delta is identically zero for the life of the position
and nothing about it degrades as price moves. Rebalance count and cost are
structurally zero and are reported as zero with that reason attached.

**Equity**, defined so nothing is counted twice:

```text
equity_t = free_cash_t + Q x spot_price_t + perp_margin + (perp_entry - perp_price_t) x Q
```

`free_cash` is the single place fees, slippage and funding reach equity. They are
also accumulated separately *for reporting*, and those accumulators are never
added to equity again. At entry, equity equals total capital minus the two legs'
entry frictions and nothing else — which the accounting controls assert.

**Block net PnL** is equity at the block's close **minus total starting
capital** — entry frictions are inside the numerator. Measuring from post-entry
equity instead would hide them, and the two bases differ by ~12.5 bps, which is
the scale at which the gate's strict sign and mean tests are decided.

## 7. Costs

Frozen, per leg, as fractions of notional. Binance published VIP-0 taker rates,
no BNB discount, taker on both legs because a position that must be *in* the
market cannot assume a passive fill.

| friction | rate |
| --- | --- |
| spot fee (entry, exit) | 0.001 each |
| perpetual fee (entry, exit) | 0.0005 each |
| spot slippage | 0.0005 per fill |
| perpetual slippage | 0.0005 per fill |
| rebalance cost | 0.0 — none are performed |
| financing / borrow | 0.0 — nothing is borrowed at 1x with an outright spot leg |
| idle cash yield | 0.0 |

Fees and slippage are charged on **notional**, never on quantity. That is 30 bps
round-trip on spot and 20 bps on the perpetual — stricter per leg than the 20 bps
round-trip the repository's directional research assumes for a single spot leg,
and covering two legs. Roughly **0.0025 of total capital per block**.

**No friction here is ever lowered because the strategy looks unprofitable.**

Market impact beyond the 5 bps slippage assumption is not modelled at ~500,000
USDT per leg, and is recorded as a limitation rather than argued away.

## 8. Margin and liquidation

**Primary model: portfolio (cross) collateral.** The two legs are one book — the
spot holding collateralises the perpetual short, which is how a delta-hedged
carry is actually financed. Liquidation is tested against total portfolio equity
against the maintenance requirement. At equal quantity the portfolio is
price-invariant, so price alone cannot exhaust it; only funding losses and costs
can.

**How that choice was made, disclosed.** It was selected during design after
checking, against the already-committed pre-boundary Binance **spot** snapshot,
whether a strictly isolated 1x short survives a calendar-year hold. It does not:
the repository's own `liquidation_price` puts a 1x isolated SHORT at
`entry x (2 - mmr)`, roughly a 99.6% adverse move, and BTC's intra-year high
exceeded that multiple of its first close in **2020, 2021, 2023 and 2024**. No
funding, basis, PnL or P13 return was computed — only price extrema of a source
already frozen here, strictly before the boundary.

The claim is deliberately narrow, because the check supports only a narrow one:
it establishes that the isolated model is **liquidated** in four of six blocks.
It does **not** establish the *sign* of those blocks' returns, and none was
computed — at equal quantity the spot leg's gain roughly offsets the forfeited
margin, so a liquidated block's return is dominated by funding accrued before the
liquidation instant. What the check justifies is that the isolated model
truncates the holding period in most blocks and therefore answers a different
question; it does not by itself prove that model would have failed the gate.

**The isolated case is measured, not assumed away.** It is stress **S4**,
reported per block, deliberately outside the gate, so how much the result depends
on this modelling choice is auditable rather than asserted.

**What the portfolio model assumes operationally**, stated because it is the
model's weakest assumption. Treating the spot holding as collateral for the
perpetual short requires an account arrangement that actually does that —
Binance Portfolio Margin, or an operator moving quote collateral between the spot
and USD-M wallets in time. A plain USD-M cross-margin account does **not**
automatically collateralise from spot BTC holdings. So the primary model
describes a carry book financed as one pool, which is how such a book is run, and
**not** the default retail configuration. Transfer latency, transfer limits and
Portfolio Margin eligibility are not modelled. S4 is the bound on how much this
assumption is worth.

The dry-run venue's missing liquidation engine is an engineering limitation and
is **not** permitted to become an economic assumption: P13 runs its own
liquidation check against price history. What it cannot determine — intra-hour
liquidation between grid points, the tiered maintenance schedule above tier 1,
the maintenance-amount deduction, auto-deleveraging, wallet-transfer latency — is
listed rather than papered over, and the check uses the hourly **high** of the
mark series as a conservative intra-bar touch where available, recording which
was used.

**The forced close is a different instant from the trigger.** A liquidation is
detected from within-bar action an hourly grid cannot resolve, so filling at the
trigger price would assume an execution precision the data does not support. The
forced close therefore executes at the **open of the following bar** — the first
price an operator could actually have transacted at, and a choice that errs
against the position. A fill at the trigger bar's own open would be a price
stamped an hour *before* the high that caused the liquidation: a fill preceding
its own cause. `nn/p13_carry.py` records the trigger instant and the fill instant
as two fields so an auditor can see they differ.

### 8a. Amendment A1 — a forced close with no following bar

**This is amendment A1, and it is not an original preregistration rule.** It is
`FORCED_CLOSE_WITHOUT_A_FOLLOWING_BAR` in
[`nn/p13_preregistration.py`](../nn/p13_preregistration.py), inside the hashed
payload.

**What the original text already determines, and A1 does not re-decide.** The
fill rule above is frozen, and §6's exit search is bounded by the block end and
by the research boundary. Together those already exclude every candidate price
for a liquidation triggered on a block's *final* bar: the trigger bar's own open
is acausal, a close is never a fill, and the following bar belongs to the next
block — or, for the last block, lies at or after the retired P4-HOLD instant.

**What it did not determine, and A1 does.** What such a block does to the gate.
§10 authorises exclusion only for a block that could not be **opened**, and this
one opened; §10 also says a liquidated block is *included* at its realised
return, and this one has none. §6 names the state — **UNCLOSED**, reported with
its reason rather than back-dated — and stops there. Two frozen rules point in
opposite directions and neither reaches the case, so a future run would have had
to choose between them *with the data in front of it*.

**The rule.** A liquidation trigger on a block's final quote leaves the block
**UNCLOSED**. The funding, fees and slippage incurred up to the trigger are
reported as the facts they are; the close-dependent quantities — exit basis,
basis PnL, net PnL, net return — are **not determinable** and are emitted as
`NaN` rather than zero, so a gate that ignores the flag raises instead of
averaging in a number nobody measured. The screen terminates
**`P13 ALWAYS-ON ANNUAL SPOT/PERP CARRY: INVALID`**. The same treatment applies
to the other cause of an UNCLOSED block that §6 already names.

**Why INVALID rather than excluded.** Excluding it is the flattering option: it
drops a block that was being liquidated out of G2's mean and out of G3's
worst-block test, which is exactly what §10 refuses for every other liquidated
block. INVALID forfeits the possibility of a VIABLE verdict outright, so this
amendment **can only make the verdict harder to obtain, never easier** — the
property that makes a post-freeze amendment admissible at all.

**When, and on what information.** Adopted after P13's environment-only
**NOT EVALUABLE** closure and **before any P13 economic observation of any
kind**. No block was opened, no return, funding total, basis figure or gate
condition was ever computed, and no market data informed the choice — the only
input was the shape of the frozen text. **This intentionally moves the
preregistration hash**; the superseded hash is recorded in §15 as history.

**It does not disturb the acquisition evidence.**
`artifacts/benchmark/btc_p13_carry/` was generated at `2b1b400e` under the
original design and quotes its hash. Those files are **not** regenerated and
**not** rewritten. A1 governs block execution, and the acquisition never obtained
a single row for any bar, so nothing in that evidence was produced under a rule
this changes.

### 8b. Reachability of A1 after the holding-window repair — a clarification, not an amendment

**Nothing in §8a changes, and the preregistration hash does not move.** A1's rule
is unchanged, still hashed, and still in force. What follows is an engineering
fact about which of A1's two causes the evaluator can now produce.

`nn/p13_carry.py` evaluated liquidation and the maximum adverse excursion over
`quotes[1:]` — bars 1 .. N. That is wrong at both ends: a position opened at bar
0's **open** holds through the remainder of bar 0, and a position closed at bar
N's **open** holds through nothing after it. The held windows are **bars
0 .. N-1**, and the evaluator now uses them.

The consequence for A1 is precise. A liquidation trigger can now only land on a
bar in 0 .. N-1, so the following bar always exists inside the block and the
forced close always has a permitted fill. **The liquidation route into A1 is
therefore unreachable from `evaluate_block`.** It was reachable only while the
loop also tested bar N — a bar the position had already closed at the open of —
so the "liquidation on the block's final quote" case A1 was written against was
in part an artefact of that off-by-one.

**A1 is not relaxed and its history is not rewritten.** Three things follow, and
only these three:

- A1 still governs the **other** UNCLOSED cause it already names — the block with
  no valid exit instant at or before its last hour (§6,
  `POSITION_LIFECYCLE.close_instant`). A1's own text says it "applies equally" to
  that cause, so this is the case it now primarily governs, and it belongs to the
  block runner, which does not exist yet.
- The rule keeps a **single executable definition**,
  `nn.p13_carry.unclosed_block_result`, callable by whatever produces either
  cause, so a frozen rule is not left encoded only in a branch nothing can reach.
- The `reachability` note inside the hashed A1 payload argues the liquidation
  case is reachable under **S4**. Read against the corrected window that note
  **overstates the reachability of the liquidation route**, and it is left
  untouched: it is amendment text inside the hashed payload, editing it would move
  the hash for a reason that is not a design decision, and A1's history is
  recorded rather than tidied. This paragraph is the forward-looking correction a
  future reader should carry instead.

Nothing here was chosen from data. No P13 economic observation exists, and none
informed this.

### 8b-i. Two things an independent review of the repair raised, recorded rather than settled

**The spot-close liquidation touch is not authorised by the frozen text, and this
document does not pretend otherwise.** `nn/p13_carry.py::Quote.liquidation_touch`
falls back twice: to the mark **close** when no hourly mark high is published, and
then to the **spot** close when no mark series is present at all. Only the first
fallback is frozen — §8's "the hourly **high** of the mark series ... where
available ... recording which was used". `MARK_PRICE_FALLBACK` authorises a spot
substitution *"as the funding notional base"*, and `BASIS_DEFINITION` lists the
liquidation test as a **separate** use of the mark series without extending the
substitution to it. So the third tier is unauthorised, and it is
anti-conservative: a spot close cannot see a perpetual mark spike.

It predates this repair and is **left in place**, because removing it would
decide an economic question the frozen text does not settle — which is exactly
the kind of decision §8b declines to make silently. What the repair changes is
that it can no longer happen invisibly: the touch provenance counts it as its own
tier, so a reviewer can see whether any block ever relied on it. **If a future run
would rely on it, that needs an explicit pre-economic amendment before the run,
not a decision taken with a number in view.**

Note also that `MARK_PRICE_FALLBACK.reporting_granularity` asks for a count of
**settlements** that used the substituted notional base. The touch provenance
counts **liquidation tests**, which is a different fact about a different use of
the series. Both are wanted; only the second exists today, and the first belongs
to the funding path the block runner will build.

**The A1 reachability change is verdict-relaxing, and that is worth saying
plainly.** §8b records that the liquidation route into A1 is now unreachable. A1's
own `direction_of_conservatism` says the rule "can only remove a verdict, never
produce or improve one" — so removing its reachability can only make `VIABLE`
*easier* to obtain, and the hashed `reachability` note asserting the case is
reachable under S4 is now false. This document is not hashed, so recording the
correction here does not move the preregistration hash, and A1's text is not
edited.

The argument that the change is nevertheless **forced rather than chosen**: a
liquidation test *at* instant `t_N` is not implementable from the preregistered
sources. Every price a test at `t_N` could use — the bar's high, its mark close,
its spot close — is a post-open fact about a bar the position closed at the open
of, and no mark **open** exists in any preregistered source. §8's own
`liquidation_check` is evaluated "while the position is open", and the position is
not held through bar `N`. So there is no causal test to perform at `t_N`, and its
absence is a consequence of the fill rule rather than a second decision.

That argument is recorded here so a reviewer can reject it. **If it is rejected,
the correct remedy is an explicit pre-economic amendment moving the hash — not a
silent restoration of the old window, which was acausal in the other direction.**

### 8c. "Following bar" when the grid has a hole

Recorded because a future run needs it settled and the frozen text does settle
it: **the following bar is the next VALID EXECUTABLE OBSERVATION, not the next
exact hourly grid instant.** No amendment is required, because this is deduction
from rules already frozen rather than a choice:

- §8's forced-close rule calls the next open "the first price an operator could
  actually have transacted at". An operator cannot transact at a missing or
  invalid grid point.
- All three lifecycle instants in `POSITION_LIFECYCLE` resolve an invalid grid
  point the same way: the open is "the **first** hourly grid instant ... at which
  observations are all present and valid", and an invalid close moves to "the
  **first valid instant at or after** it — an operator who cannot trade keeps
  holding".
- `validity_definition` makes an invalid instant fail closed. Filling at one would
  contradict the rule that a fill is a knowable, present open.

The bound is unchanged: the search still may not run past the block end or the
research boundary.

What this **does** require is that a hole stop being invisible. A multi-hour jump
must not read as an ordinary `+1h` transition, so `BlockResult` now records
`quote_gap_count`, `max_quote_step_ns` and `forced_close_gap_ns` — the last being
how far a forced close's fill actually lay from its trigger. The economics are
unchanged by this; only the evidence gets richer. `SOURCE_FREEZE_FIELDS` already
requires "gaps detected" of the acquisition, and this is the same fact recorded on
the evaluation side.

## 9. Temporal partition

Six UTC calendar-year blocks: **2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025-partial**
(the last running to the boundary, 4.6 months).

A calendar year spans multiple funding regimes, which is exactly what a
structural carry screen must not slice away.

**What was and was not chosen**, stated rather than overclaimed: the *outer*
edges are not a choice — the span starts where the sources begin and ends at the
research boundary. The five *interior* boundaries **are** a choice, and because
each block opens and closes a position they fix ten of the twelve fill instants,
so block returns are sensitive to them. Saying "no boundary was selected" would
be false; what is true is that the convention was fixed before any result and
cannot be moved after one. D3 reports how much it mattered. It is deliberately
**not**
P6's four-fold gate: funding carry has a different event structure, and copying
that partition because it existed would be borrowing a design rather than
choosing one.

One position per block: one open, one close, zero rebalances, and no inventory
carried across blocks, so a bad block cannot hide inside a neighbour.

**The block is the unit of inference, and there are six.** The thousands of
individual funding payments are *not* thousands of independent experiments, and
are reported as a diagnostic count.

Forbidden: splitting a block after seeing it, excluding negative-funding regimes,
slicing one regime finely to manufacture independence.

## 10. The viability gate — frozen before the first number

| condition | rule |
| --- | --- |
| **G1** breadth | net block return `> 0` in at least **4 of 6** blocks |
| **G2** central tendency | mean net block return `> 0` |
| **G3** downside | worst block `>= -0.02` of total capital |
| **G4** sample | every included block has `>= 200` settlements, and `>= 5` blocks included |
| **G5** stress | **S1** and **S3** must each also satisfy G1 and G2; G3 must hold under S1 |
| **G6** effect size | mean net block return `> 0.0025` |

**All six. Any single failure is a FAIL.**

Where the numbers come from — mechanism and the frozen cost model, never an
observed return:

- **4 of 6** is two-thirds rather than a bare majority, and deliberately not a
  copy of P6's 3-of-4. A structural mechanism that pays in only half its years is
  not a carry.
- **-0.02** is roughly eight times a block's round-trip friction: a bad year may
  materially exceed costs without being catastrophic for something described as
  delta-neutral carry.
- **200 settlements** is about two months of continuous 8-hourly funding.
- **0.0025** is exactly one block's round-trip friction. Without it the gate
  could emit VIABLE on a mean of a few basis points a year — economically
  indistinguishable from zero and an order of magnitude below the frictions
  already modelled.
- **S3 is in the gate** because this construction performs one open and one close
  per block, so doubling frictions moves a block by a fixed ~25 bps once a year:
  S1 alone is the stress this design is *structurally least sensitive to*, and
  gating on it alone would read as robustness while buying almost none. S3
  perturbs the basis, which is the variable the payoff identity is built on.

**Ties**, resolved in every direction: a block return of exactly zero is not
positive; a mean of exactly zero fails G2; a mean of exactly 0.0025 fails G6; a
worst block of exactly -0.02 passes G3; exactly 200 settlements passes G4.

**Horizon convention**, declared rather than corrected: block returns are per
block period and are **not** annualised. G1, G2 and G3 operate unweighted, so the
4.6-month 2025 block carries the same weight in the mean as a full year and the
-0.02 floor is a looser annualised bar for it. Renormalising would move a frozen
threshold; the disparity is a property of where the sources end.

**Operating characteristic**, stated so the gate is not read as stronger than it
is: preregistration protects against selection, it does not create power. Against
a symmetric coin-flip null over six blocks, **G1 alone admits about 34% of
outcomes** — G1 is not the discriminating condition, the conjunction is. With six
inferential units this screen can falsify convincingly and can only ever weakly
support.

**Excluded blocks.** A block that could not be opened is excluded from G1 and G2
and reported with its reason. Fewer than five remaining makes the screen
**INVALID** rather than PASS or FAIL. Liquidated blocks are *included* at their
realised return.

## 11. Stress cases

| id | definition | role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S0 | the frozen cost model | base |
| S1 | every fee and slippage rate doubled | **in the gate** (G5) |
| S2 | legs not simultaneous — evaluated **both orderings**, worse reported | diagnostic |
| S3 | entry basis **reduced** and exit basis **increased** by 10 bps of spot | **in the gate** (G5) |
| S4 | strict isolated margin: funding debits the **isolated balance**, no rescue | diagnostic, per block |

S2 is two-sided deliberately: in a rising sample a one-sided hedging delay is a
*benefit*, and reporting only that ordering would dress a windfall as a
robustness check. S3's direction is stated because "worsened" is not a direction;
its total bite is ~0.0010 of capital against S1's ~0.0025. S4 debits funding from
the walled-off balance rather than from portfolio cash, because a short paying
funding for months that never feels it in the isolated balance is not a strict
bound at all.

### Payoff-side diagnostics — predeclared, never gating

Every stress above perturbs the **cost** side. None perturbs the **payoff** side,
in a sample era whose funding was historically elevated — the same era the leg
direction was chosen knowing about. Three diagnostics are therefore frozen here,
with no thresholds and no bearing on the gate:

| id | definition | what it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | every realised funding rate x 0.5 | how much survives if the era's funding were half as rich |
| D2 | leave-one-block-out mean, all six | whether the aggregate rests on one exceptional year |
| D3 | the same rule on blocks offset six months | how much the interior partition boundaries mattered |

They are reported, never gated on, and never used to select anything. A
disappointing base result is not rescued by a flattering diagnostic, and a
flattering base result is not defended by ignoring an unflattering one.

Stress cases are diagnostics. They are not a parameter surface, the best of them
is never promoted, and the strategy is never re-specified to survive one. The
10 bps S3 magnitude is frozen here and is never revised against an observed basis
series.

Note that the basis identity — price PnL `= Q x (basis_in - basis_out)` — holds
under S0, S1 and S3 but **not** under S2, where the legs are deliberately not
simultaneous. The invariant control is scoped accordingly; asserting it under S2
would either fail spuriously or, worse, invite weakening the accounting.

## 12. Stopping rules

**If VIABLE:** stop the alpha search. A pass establishes only that this
preregistered historical mechanism survived one exploratory screen under these
assumptions. It authorises no real money, no Styx read, no leverage above 1x, no
live futures routing, no parameter expansion, no promotion. Close and audit
first.

**If NOT VIABLE:** stop. Publish the negative. Do not change thresholds, the
funding rule, the rebalance cadence, the costs, the leverage or the venue; do not
add market making; do not write P13-v2 in the task that produced v1; do not
inspect Styx or P4-HOLD.

**If INVALID or NOT EVALUABLE:** record it with the evidence establishing it. The
design stays frozen and executable. NOT EVALUABLE is terminal for this design,
not an invitation to redesign it against whatever data happens to be reachable.

**What a negative would and would not mean.** A NOT VIABLE verdict would say that
*this* construction — always-on, one position per calendar year,
quantity-matched, 1x, taker on both legs, Binance only — did not clear this gate
on 2020–2025 history. It would **not** say that funding/basis carry is
unprofitable: a shorter holding period, a maker execution assumption, a causal
funding filter, a different venue pair or a different hedge instrument are all
untested by it, and each would be a separate preregistration. The result labels
name the construction for exactly this reason.

## 13. What the run must leave behind

A summary JSON is not enough. An independent reviewer must be able to
reimplement the accounting loop from the primary evidence **without importing
this checkpoint's evaluator**, and that is only possible if every *event* is
recorded rather than every *total*.

- **Primary evidence** is an event-level ledger under
  `artifacts/benchmark/btc_p13_carry/`: one row per position open, close, funding
  settlement applied, fee, slippage charge and liquidation test that fired, plus
  the per-instant equity series. Each row carries the quantity, both leg prices,
  the mark, the basis, the signed cash flow and the resulting equity — so block
  totals are **recomputable from it** rather than asserted by it.
- **It has its own manifest**, separate from the source manifests. Derived
  reports — per-block tables, the decision aggregate, any prose summary — are
  regenerable *from* the primary evidence and are pinned by regenerating them and
  checking what they say, per [`artifacts/README.md`](../artifacts/README.md).
- **Source manifests** are one per acquired archive object, carrying every field
  in `SOURCE_FREEZE_FIELDS`. A source manifest is not the evidence manifest and
  neither substitutes for the other.
- **Provenance** is recorded on each artifact: the preregistration hash, the git
  revision, and whether the tree was dirty. A dirty tree refuses to generate
  primary evidence at all.
- **Stress artifacts** S1–S4 are written beside the base result, each labelled by
  stress id. They are diagnostics and are never presented as the result.

## 14. Provenance and safety

**Primary result generation must begin from a clean, committed source tree.** A
run from a dirty tree is refused rather than annotated. This exists because P6's
fifteen primary cells record `dirty: true` and their fits are consequently not
reconstructible from a clean checkout — a disclosed defect this checkpoint does
not repeat.

**Tripwire.** No P13 result artifact may exist when this preregistration is
committed. `tests/test_p13_preregistration.py` asserts that
`artifacts/benchmark/btc_p13_decision/decision.json` is absent, so a
preregistration commit silently carrying a result fails the suite.

Non-negotiable: no real money; no authenticated live futures order route and no
expansion of live reachability; no leverage above 1x; no P4-HOLD read; no Styx
read; no manufactured holdout; no live Freqtrade experiment; no operational PnL
selecting alpha; no parameter shopping after results; no rewriting of P1–P8
frozen evidence. Aegis remains the central risk authority, and risk-reducing
close and emergency-flatten paths remain available under halt.
