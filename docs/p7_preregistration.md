## 1. The question

> Does causal agreement between independently trained temporal specialists add
> robust cost-aware value beyond the corresponding individual specialists?

P6 screened five specialists **individually** and found none of them viable. P7
asks a different question about the same objects: whether *agreement between*
them is worth more than any of them alone. Agreement is not a member of the set
it is computed from — a filter that trades only where independently fitted
models concur can in principle be better or worse than each of them — so a
negative P6 makes this question more interesting rather than moot.

**No hypothesis is asserted.** Neither direction is claimed.

## 2. What P7 fits: nothing

P7 replays the **frozen P6 XGBoost specialists' committed per-sample
predictions**:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| cells | `artifacts/benchmark/btc_p6_{clock}_xgboost/outer_predictions.parquet` |
| frozen under | `artifacts/btc_p6_SHA256SUMS.txt` |
| column read | `selected_action` |
| P6 preregistration | `sha256:2785b1a7b19ecceca58cd0e936d14a5cbbbe6eb10f7ddf2796800409a0eaaaf2` |

`selected_action` is each specialist's own predetermined decision, produced by
its own frozen threshold on its own inner block during P6. **P7 reads the action
and never the probabilities**, so there is no second threshold anywhere in this
checkpoint.

No specialist is refitted, no threshold is re-selected, no probability is
re-scored and no weight is searched.

**The specialist sets are architectural, not chosen.** A scalping decision reads
fast clocks and a day-trading decision reads intraday ones; that is what the
modes *are*. P6 was negative on all five clocks, so there was no attractive
subset to pick even if picking were allowed — and P6 §9.4 predeclared that P7
must not depend on which specialists cleared an absolute floor.

## 3. Causal alignment

**The reference instant is the decision bar's close.** A row's trade is entered
at its close, because the label is `close[t+6]/close[t]-1`. Everything that had
closed at or before that instant is available; nothing else is.

```
reference_instant = decision_bar_open + one decision-clock bar
as_of = searchsorted(specialist_close_times, reference_instant, side="right") - 1
specialist_close_time = specialist_bar_open + that specialist's bar width
```

`side="right"` means a decision row landing **exactly** on a specialist close
*does* see that bar — it has printed. This is the same boundary convention
`nn.mtf` uses, and the boundary rows are the only ones where a shift is
detectable at all.

Never: a prediction whose source candle had not closed, a forward fill across a
specialist's missing bar, or an interpolation of any kind.

**On its own decision clock the rule is the identity**, because a bar closes at
its own close. That is not assumed — §6 turns it into the validity gate.

### 3.1 When a specialist has nothing yet

At the head of an outer block a slower specialist has not yet closed a bar
inside it. The rule is strict: **if any required specialist is unavailable, the
consensus is HOLD**, and that constituent's own replay is HOLD on that row. A
partial vote would let a rule reach its threshold on whichever members happen to
be present, which makes the result a function of where a block starts.

Measured from the committed P6 predictions, before any consensus existed:

| mode | decision rows | unavailable rows | worst fold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| scalping | 1,161,875 | `1m` 0, `5m` 16, `15m` 56 | 14 |
| day trading | 232,285 | `5m` 0, `15m` 8, `30m` 20, `1h` 44 | 11 |

Every one is at the head of an outer block. The largest loss on any fold is 14
rows of 289,248, so the unavailability rule cannot carry a result either way.

## 4. The two modes

Two experiments, **two verdicts, reported separately**.

### 4.1 P7A — scalping consensus

| | |
| --- | --- |
| decision clock | `1m` |
| specialists | `1m`, `5m`, `15m` |
| veto specialist | `15m` |
| horizon | 6 decision bars = 6 minutes |

> **LONG** when at least **2 of the 3** specialists are actively LONG **and** the
> `15m` specialist is **not** actively SHORT.
> **SHORT** when at least **2 of the 3** are actively SHORT **and** the `15m`
> specialist is **not** actively LONG.
> Otherwise **HOLD**.

### 4.2 P7B — day-trading consensus

| | |
| --- | --- |
| decision clock | `5m` |
| specialists | `5m`, `15m`, `30m`, `1h` |
| veto specialist | `1h` |
| horizon | 6 decision bars = 30 minutes |

> **LONG** when at least **3 of the 4** specialists are actively LONG **and** the
> `1h` specialist is **not** actively SHORT.
> **SHORT** when at least **3 of the 4** are actively SHORT **and** the `1h`
> specialist is **not** actively LONG.
> Otherwise **HOLD**.

"Actively LONG" and "actively SHORT" mean the specialist's own frozen action.
HOLD is neither, so a HOLD never vetoes and never votes.

### 4.3 What the rules may not become

No discretionary weighting — every vote counts exactly one. No weight search, no
threshold search, no agreement-count search. No per-fold or per-mode variation.
And no `consensus_v2` in the task that produced `consensus_v1`'s result.

## 5. Controls — the comparison that makes this fair

A consensus taken every minute **cannot** be compared against a specialist's
native P6 return. Those were measured under a different execution cadence: the
`15m` specialist in P6 took a position every fifteen minutes at most, and here it
is asked at every minute.

So **every constituent is replayed on the mode's own decision clock**, through
the same causal alignment and the same accounting as the consensus:

- `nn.evaluate.trading_metrics` on the decision clock's own `future_return` and
  row index;
- the same greedy non-overlapping trade rule and the same six-native-bar hold;
- the same 20 bps round trip (`fee_rate` 0.0005, `slippage_rate` 0.0005) charged
  once per realised trade.

Costs are unchanged from P6 and from every checkpoint since v4. Reducing them
because a 1m consensus otherwise looks bad is forbidden; turnover and trade count
are reported for the consensus and for every constituent.

### 5.1 The fold-wise best constituent

For each outer fold:

```
best_constituent = max over the mode's constituents of that constituent's
                   replayed outer net return, within that fold
fold_delta       = consensus net return - best_constituent net return
```

The best constituent is computed **mechanically inside each fold** and is a
**benchmark, not a selection**: nothing is deployed, carried forward or preferred
by winning a fold. Comparing against the fold-wise best is deliberately the
hardest fair bar — a consensus that only beats the *average* of its members has
not shown it is worth the machinery.

## 6. Validity gate, checked before any delta is read

The alignment maps a mode's own decision clock onto itself as the identity.
Therefore replaying that specialist through P7's machinery **must reproduce the
frozen P6 cell's four outer net returns exactly**, to the 1e-6 rounding
`trading_metrics` applies.

If it does not, **P7 is invalid**: no verdict is issued and no delta is reported.
It is the one property that ties P7's accounting to P6's, and a replay that
cannot reproduce the specialist it claims to be replaying is measuring something
else.

## 7. The decision rule

Applied **per mode, separately**. Supportive adaptive evidence requires **both**:

1. the consensus beats the fold-wise best constituent in at least **3 of the 4**
   outer folds;
2. the **mean fold delta** across the four folds is **> 0**.

These two, and nothing else. A criterion added after the deltas are visible is
not a criterion — it is a description of the result.

### 7.1 The possible answers

- both modes supportive;
- scalping supportive only;
- day trading supportive only;
- neither supportive;
- invalid.

The two modes are reported separately and neither is collapsed into the other.
"Scalping supportive, day trading not" is a result, **not** a reason to report
scalping as P7's answer.

### 7.2 Evidence ceiling

**Exploratory, adaptive, and a rung lower than P6's.** These are the same four
real-world windows v4, P2a, P2b, P2c, P3, P4, P5 and P6 have read — the ninth
reading — and P7 is designed with P6's results already known. No P7 result is
confirmatory. A positive P7 would need confirmation these blocks cannot supply;
a negative one needs no discounting.

### 7.3 What each outcome licenses

- **Supportive.** Adaptive evidence that agreement added value over its own
  components on four burned blocks. Not a deployable strategy, not out-of-sample
  evidence, and not permission to trade real money.
- **Negative.** Recorded as negative. The trading-mode scaffold may still
  *describe* the mode and must not claim alpha for it.
- **Invalid.** The validity gate failed; no verdict until it is fixed.

### 7.4 Styx and P4-HOLD

P7 reads **no candle at all** — only committed P6 predictions, which exist solely
for rows inside the four outer blocks. The sealed region is not reachable from
this checkpoint by any path, and P4-HOLD stays retired with `checkpoint: null`.

## 8. Forbidden once any valid P7 result exists

- changing either consensus rule, its agreement count or its veto specialist;
- changing either mode's specialist set or decision clock;
- refitting, re-thresholding or re-weighting any specialist;
- changing the alignment rule or the unavailability rule;
- changing the cost model, the fee rate or the slippage rate;
- changing the control definition or the fold-wise best-constituent benchmark;
- changing either condition of the decision rule, or adding a third;
- reporting one mode's verdict as P7's answer;
- writing `consensus_v2` in the task that produced `consensus_v1`'s result.

## 9. Causality — the consensus battery

Each item carries a positive control that deliberately introduces the fault and
asserts the check catches it.

| id | property | positive control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | no decision row reads a specialist bar that had not closed | a specialist prediction shifted one bar earlier is caught |
| C2 | a row landing exactly on a specialist close does see that bar | the boundary row is constructed and asserted in both directions |
| C3 | the own-clock alignment is the identity | asserted per mode; the validity gate depends on it |
| C4 | a missing specialist yields HOLD and never a partial vote | a specialist truncated at the block head is asserted to HOLD |
| C5 | duplicate specialist predictions for one bar are refused | a duplicated row is injected and the loader raises |
| C6 | the consensus is deterministic | the same frame is replayed twice and the digests compared |
| C7 | HOLD propagates — no agreement, no position | an all-HOLD frame yields zero trades |
| C8 | the veto blocks agreement it disagrees with | a 2-of-3 LONG with a SHORT veto yields HOLD |
| C9 | disagreement yields HOLD | one LONG, one SHORT, one HOLD yields HOLD |
| C10 | same-timestamp ordering does not affect the result | a shuffled input frame reproduces the same consensus |

### Four things about this result

**Agreement mostly removed trades rather than improving them.** The scalping
consensus took 9 to 31 trades per fold where its own 1m constituent took 16 to
46, and the day-trading consensus took 0 to 6 where its 5m constituent took 8 to
43. Requiring a majority *and* clearing a veto is a filter, and on these folds
the trades it removed were, on balance, the ones worth keeping.

**Day-trading fold 3 took no trade at all.** Over 57,840 five-minute decision
rows, three of four specialists never agreed with the `1h` specialist not
opposing. A consensus that cannot find a position is a real outcome of the rule
and is reported as `0.000000` — a flat quarter, not a missing number. It is also
the single largest contributor to that mode's negative mean, and no condition
was relaxed for it.

**The bar was deliberately the hardest fair one.** The comparison is against the
*fold-wise best* constituent, chosen mechanically inside each fold after the
fact. Against a weaker benchmark — the average constituent, or a fixed one named
in advance — scalping would look better on more folds. §5.1 predeclared the
fold-wise best precisely so that "beats one of its members" could not be
reported as "adds value", and it is not being relaxed now that it bound.

**The one improvement is one fold.** Scalping's fold 3 delta is `+0.009321` and
day trading's fold 0 is `+0.052893`. Two positive folds across eight is what the
3-of-4 condition exists to refuse, and it refused them.

### A third thing, which the design did not think of

**The alignment has no staleness bound, and on these folds a vote can be almost
four days old.** §4 makes a specialist available when its own bar has closed at
or before the decision instant, and §4's `UNAVAILABILITY_RULE` covers only the
case where *no* bar has closed yet. Neither says how old the last closed bar may
be. It matters because the P6 prediction series have gaps: the shared warm-up and
label embargo are applied per contiguous segment, so an exchange outage removes a
run of bars from a specialist's series, and on a slow clock that run is long.

Measured on the committed artifacts, over every decision row of all four folds:

| mode | specialist | rows voting on a bar older than one of its own | worst age |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| scalping | `5m` | 429 of 1,161,859 (0.037%) | 9h 29m |
| scalping | `15m` | 1,564 of 1,161,819 (0.135%) | 1d 0h 14m |
| day trading | `15m` | 222 of 232,277 (0.096%) | 1d 0h 10m |
| day trading | `30m` | 570 of 232,265 (0.245%) | 1d 22h 55m |
| day trading | `1h` | 1,272 of 232,241 (0.548%) | **3d 20h 55m** |

The worst case is the `1h` specialist in day trading, which is also that mode's
**veto**. On a decision clock's own clock the age is always zero, which is the
identity §6 checks.

**This is not fixed here.** A staleness bound is a change to the alignment rule,
and adding one after the deltas are visible is exactly what §9 forbids — it would
turn a measured result into a chosen one. It is recorded as a limitation of P7 v1
and as the first thing a P7 v2 would specify **before** looking at anything, and
it does not rescue either verdict: both modes failed, and a rule that voted on
fresher information might have failed differently but was not the rule that ran.
`tests/test_p7_consensus.py::test_the_measured_staleness_is_the_one_this_document_publishes`
recomputes the table.

### The two diagnostics this document promised and the artifacts do not carry

§8's `trade_count` diagnostic says trade counts are "flagged below 10 outer
trades, changes no denominator". The counts are reported per fold in both mode
artifacts, but **no flag field was ever emitted**, so the flag is recorded here
instead: it fires on **scalping fold 1** (9 trades) and on **all four
day-trading folds** (6, 1, 6, 0). Five of the eight folds carrying these two
verdicts are below the threshold the design set for taking a fold seriously.
That does not move a condition — the rule counts folds, and it was applied — but
a reader who takes P7B's `-0.034336` as a measurement of anything should know it
rests on thirteen realised trades.

§6's validity gate is the second. It asks that the mode's own decision-clock
specialist "reproduce the frozen P6 cell's four outer net returns exactly", and
`nn.p7.validity_gate` instead asserts the alignment is the identity — the action
array P7 replays is the action array P6 selected, row for row. That implies the
promised equality only if P7's accounting is P6's, which the proxy does not
establish. The promised comparison is therefore made directly against the
committed artifacts, in
`tests/test_p7_evidence.py::test_the_preregistered_validity_gate_holds_on_the_frozen_evidence`,
and **it holds on both modes**: `[-0.048409, +0.080876, -0.016257, +0.104138]`
for scalping's `1m` and `[-0.007995, +0.073974, -0.078310, +0.058097]` for day
trading's `5m`, equal to their frozen P6 cells to the last digit. P7 is valid as
preregistered; what was missing was the check, not the property.

### What this licenses, and what it does not

> Under this design — the frozen P6 XGBoost specialists, `consensus_v1`'s
> majority-plus-veto rules, causal last-closed alignment, a 20 bps round trip,
> and four adaptive temporal folds of one asset on one exchange — cross-timeframe
> agreement did not add cost-aware value over the fold-wise best of its own
> components, in either the scalping or the day-trading composition.

It is **not** proof that consensus is useless, that a different agreement count,
a different veto, a different specialist set, a different decision cadence, or a
consensus over *individually viable* specialists would fail the same way. P6 had
already found no viable clock, so this is agreement among components that were
themselves not viable — which is exactly the experiment that was preregistered,
and exactly the limit on what it can conclude.

**Do not respond to this by writing `consensus_v2`.** The rules, the agreement
counts, the vetoes, the specialist sets and the decision rule were all
predeclared, and searching any of them against these four outer blocks would
convert a negative result into a fitted one. §8 lists them.

### What survives

The machinery, which is the more durable half. `chimera/consensus.py` is a pure,
deterministic decision the live trading-mode controller calls directly, so the
scaffold that follows expresses the same rule this checkpoint measured — and,
because the answer was negative, expresses it while claiming no alpha for it.
