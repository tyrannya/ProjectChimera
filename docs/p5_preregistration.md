# P5 — preregistration

**Written before any P5 model was fitted, before any P5 arm existed in code, and
before any P5 number existed.** What *had* happened when this was frozen is
recorded in §3.6: a measurement of which rows have a higher-timeframe context at
all, computed from timestamps and bar completeness without fitting anything. That
measurement carries availability information and no outcome information, and it
is the reason the availability rule below can be stated as a fact rather than as
a hope.

Research checkpoint: **P5** (*does strictly causal higher-timeframe OHLCV context
provide robust incremental information beyond the current 1h OHLCV14 baseline, in
the unchanged BTC/USDT 1h-observation / 6-candle-horizon cost-aware setup?*)

## 1. The question, and why this axis

Four checkpoints have now asked whether some family of features adds information
beyond OHLCV14, and four have answered no: `smc_v1` (P2b), `chart_structure_v1`
(P2c), `microstructure_v1` (P3) and `derivatives_v1` (P4). P3's own write-up
named what those four have in common and what they left untested:

> It did **not** establish that microstructure is generally useless, that L1/L2
> order-flow imbalance is useless, that shorter-horizon trade flow is useless, or
> that **multi-timeframe information is useless**. None of those was tested.

Every family so far was computed on **one clock**. `smc_v1` and
`chart_structure_v1` transform the 1h bar; `microstructure_v1` folds trades into
1h buckets; `derivatives_v1` resamples funding, open interest and basis onto the
1h grid. A decision taken at 14:00 has never been allowed to see what the 4h bar
or the day did, as its own bar, on its own clock.

P5 changes exactly that and nothing else.

**Hypothesis.** If trend and volatility context on a 4h and a daily clock carry
information the 1h OHLCV14 vector does not, adding them improves the cost-aware
net return of the deciding model in most temporal folds. If they do not, they do
not.

**Why this axis rather than a fifth handcrafted family.** Because it is not a
fifth transformation of the same numbers. It is the same transformation of
*different* numbers — bars nobody in this programme has ever computed — and it is
the cheapest untested axis that requires no new source, no new asset and no new
horizon. It also does not need a new acquisition: §2 reads the OHLCV snapshot
already committed.

## 2. Source and sample universe

the control is built from — and derives its higher-timeframe bars from it. That

new data at all.

- exchange `binance`, pair `BTC/USDT`, base timeframe `1h`;
- research contract `btc-usdt-1h-gen1`, contract hash
  `dca6d0a891a257197a7c8aecec04fdba0a0b3009cfe93c5f3a397d458ab4a1de`;

## 3. Feature spec — `mtf_v1`

### 3.1 Timeframes and the bar grid

Two higher timeframes, both cut on a **fixed UTC grid** rather than on a rolling
window:

| timeframe | width | bars begin at | column prefix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `4h` | 4 hours | 00, 04, 08, 12, 16, 20 UTC | `mtf_4h_` |
| `1d` | 24 hours | 00 UTC | `mtf_1d_` |

A fixed grid and not a rolling window, because the grid is what the venue itself
publishes and the only one a live system could reproduce without knowing when it
happened to start. A rolling 4-hour aggregate anchored on the current row is a
different object with a different answer, and choosing between them after seeing
results would be a search.

### 3.2 Bar construction, and what a bar is not

    open   = first 1h open in the bar
    high   = max   1h high in the bar
    low    = min   1h low  in the bar
    close  = last  1h close in the bar
    volume = sum   1h volume in the bar

**A higher-timeframe bar is used only if every one of its constituent 1h candles
is present.** An incomplete bar is **dropped entirely**. It is never partially
formed, never forward-filled, never approximated from the hours that are there.
This is the rule that makes "fully closed" mean something on a punctured feed:
the committed history has 15 gaps totalling 32 missing hours, and a bar built
from three of its four hours is not a 4h bar.

### 3.3 The feature engine, unchanged

`chimera.features.compute_features` — the OHLCV14 engine the control is built
from — applied to the complete-bar series with `FeatureSpec()`, the defaults,
unchanged. Fourteen columns per timeframe, twenty-eight in total, named
`mtf_4h_<name>` and `mtf_1d_<name>` for each of:

    ret_1, log_ret_1, ret_close_open, hl_range, ema_fast_ratio, ema_slow_ratio,
    ema_cross, rsi_centered, macd_norm, macd_hist_norm, atr_norm, realized_vol,
    volume_change, volume_z

**The window lengths are counts of bars, not hours, and they are not rescaled.**
A 26-bar EMA on 4h bars is a 104-hour lookback where the same constant is a
26-hour lookback on the 1h series — and that *is the arm*. Rescaling the windows
so the lookback in hours matched would make the higher-timeframe arm a smoothed
copy of the control, which is a question about smoothing rather than about
timeframe.

No OHLCV14 feature is inherently 1h-specific. Every one is a function of the
five OHLCV numbers of a bar and its predecessors, with all constants expressed in
bars. That is checked rather than asserted: `tests/test_p5_leakage.py` recomputes
the family and compares it against `compute_features` run directly on the bar
series.

### 3.4 Warm-up

The first **78** bars of each higher-timeframe complete-bar series are not usable.
78 is `FeatureSpec.warmup`, and it is the same number and the same reason the 1h
spine itself was built with: `nn.data_pipeline.build_dataset` drops `warmup` rows
at the head of each segment because Wilder- and EMA-smoothed quantities converge
rather than becoming exact at a fixed row.

### 3.5 Alignment — what a 1h row is allowed to see

> At the 1h row timestamped `t`, the context is the **last complete
> higher-timeframe bar whose close time is at or before `t`**.

A bar beginning at `s` with width `w` closes at `s + w`. A bar that has not
closed at `t` is never visible at `t`, at any row, ever.

**One-bar staleness bound.** The as-of bar must be the *immediately preceding*
complete bar: `t − close_time < w`. A row whose nearest complete bar is older
than that — because a bar between them was dropped as incomplete — is
**ineligible** rather than served stale context. Fail closed: the alternative is
a row silently reading a 4h context from eight hours ago while every neighbouring
row reads a fresh one.

### 3.6 Contiguity — the one real decision, and the measurement that settled it

The complete-bar series is treated as **contiguous** when features are computed:
feature state is not reset at a dropped bar. The cost is that a handful of
bar-to-bar differences span two bar widths rather than one — 20 of 11,792 4h bars
and 16 of 1,966 1d bars are dropped.

The alternative considered was a **per-segment reset**, matching how `smc_v1` and
`chart_structure_v1` reset their state at a market-data gap. It was rejected on a
measurement taken before any fit:

| policy | eligible spine rows | outer block 0 | fold 1 inner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| contiguous | 44,171 / 45,802 = **0.964** | **1.000** | **1.000** |
| per-segment reset | 30,563 / 45,802 = 0.667 | 0.621 | 0.621 |

A per-segment reset does not merely cost sample. It fails the availability rule
in §6, which means it **changes which folds exist** — and "3 of 4" would then be
a statement about a different experiment. Choosing the policy that keeps four
folds is a decision made on availability grounds, before any model was fitted,
and it is recorded here so that it cannot later look like a choice made because
of what the folds said.

**Why dropping a bar is not the leak the reset exists to prevent.** `smc_v1` and
`chart_structure_v1` reset at a gap because their state is *structural* — a swing
high inferred across a hole is a claim about prices nobody saw. A moving average
over observed closes makes no such claim. Every bar that survives the
completeness rule is fully observed and strictly in the past of the row that
reads it; dropping a bar removes an unobserved bar from a series, it does not
invent one.

## 4. Causality

### 4.1 What could leak, precisely

Three things, and each has a check and a positive control in §4.4:

1. **A bar that has not closed.** The 4h bar covering 12:00–16:00 must be
   invisible to the rows at 12:00, 13:00, 14:00 and 15:00, and visible from 16:00.
   Getting this wrong is a four-hour look-ahead on fourteen columns at once, and
   for the daily bar a twenty-four-hour one on fourteen more.
2. **A join off by one bar.** The as-of index landing one bar late is the same
   leak in a different disguise, and it is invisible to every other check in
   `prove_alignment` because every other array is shared between the views by
   object identity.
3. **A feature computed from the future of the series.** A centred window or a
   backfill anywhere in the engine would put later bars into an earlier row's
   value without moving the join at all.

### 4.2 Why the boundary is the interesting case

Within a bar, every 1h row reads the same higher-timeframe context, so the joined
column is piecewise constant across `w` consecutive rows. That has a consequence
for how the join is checked: **a ±1-row shift of a piecewise-constant column
matches almost everywhere**, so the standard shift control this repository uses
for `smc_body_ratio` and `ms_qty_imbalance` is degenerate here and would report
a clean join whether or not the join was clean.

The check is therefore evaluated at the **boundary rows** — the rows where the
as-of bar index changes — where a shift is detectable, and the count of such rows
is reported so nobody has to take the coverage on trust.

### 4.3 What P5 does not touch

- **Labels.** `targets`, `future_return`, `close`, `dates` and `segment_ids`
  are the spine's own arrays, shared between every arm by object identity.
- **The control.** The `ohlcv14` arm is the spine's own fourteen columns.
- **Styx.** No higher-timeframe bar is built from an hour at or after
  `2025-08-27T23:00:00+00:00`, because the raw history is truncated at the
  spine's last row before any bar is cut.
- **P4-HOLD.** For the same reason. Rows `[45802, 48211)` are past the
  truncation point and no bar reaches them.

### 4.4 The leakage battery — what must pass before any cell is scored

Every item has a **positive control**: a test that deliberately introduces the
leak and asserts the check catches it. A check that has never failed is not
evidence.

| id | must show | positive control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | no not-yet-closed 4h bar is used | advance the as-of index one bar; the check must fail |
| L2 | the same for 1d | advance the as-of index one bar; the check must fail |
| L3 | boundary timestamps are causal: a row landing exactly on a close sees that bar and not the next | shift the close-time convention one bar width; the boundary rows must change |
| L4 | no bar is built from an hour at or after Styx, or from an hour inside P4-HOLD | extend the source past the truncation point; the check must fail |
| L5 | labels are unchanged, shared by object identity | `prove_alignment` already raises on a view that does not share them |
| L6 | the control's columns are byte-identical to the spine's own | perturb one control column; the check must fail |
| L7 | every arm scores the same rows in every block of every fold | `prove_alignment` already raises on differing sample indices |
| L8 | nothing is forward-filled from a future close; the as-of index is monotone and every as-of bar closed in the past | roll the joined column by −1 across a bar boundary; the check must fail |
| L9 | a deliberately future-shifted `mtf_v1` is detected | build the family from the NEXT bar; every causal check must fail |
| L10 | causality is structural: the family computed on the full raw history and on the truncated history agree on every spine row | a centred window would disagree; the test constructs one and asserts the check catches it |

## 6. Sample universe and availability

### 6.1 Eligibility

A spine row is **eligible** — may be scored at all — only if:

1. an as-of complete 4h bar exists at or before it;
2. an as-of complete 1d bar exists at or before it;
3. each as-of bar is at index ≥ 78 in its own complete-bar series;
4. each as-of bar satisfies the one-bar staleness bound of §3.5;
5. all 28 `mtf_v1` columns are finite at the row.

The universe is **computed once and applied to all three arms from the same array
object**, so "the arms were compared on the same rows" is a property of
construction rather than a claim. That is the mechanism
[`p4_preregistration.md`](p4_preregistration.md) §6.2 established, reused
unchanged: comparing an arm scored where its data exists against a control scored
everywhere would measure two market periods and report the difference as an
information set.

### 6.2 Block availability

| condition | value |
| --- | --- |
| minimum eligible-row fraction | **0.98** |
| maximum contiguous ineligible run | **48 hours** |
| applied to | each fold's **inner-validation** and **outer-validation** block |

The shape and both numbers are P4's `BLOCK_AVAILABILITY_RULE`, reused verbatim.
What is new is the inner block. The decision threshold is selected there, so a
punctured inner block would select a threshold on a different period than the
fold reports on. P4 had no inner-block condition; this is a **strengthening**, and
it is declared here before any fit rather than after seeing what it costs.

**Training blocks are reported, never gating.** Training loss is identical across
arms by construction, and requiring 98% of a training block that begins at the
dataset's own start would disqualify every fold over a warm-up cost that is a
property of the start date rather than of the fold.

### 6.3 The availability gate — all four folds, or nothing

**P5 requires all 4 of 4 folds to qualify.** If fewer do, the outcome is
`not_evaluable`: P5 reports invalid/unanswered, it does **not** re-derive a bar
over the surviving folds, and it does **not** drop a fold.

P4 could tolerate three of four because its availability was genuinely unknown
when it was written — the archive's inception date had not been established.
P5's availability is measurable before any fit from timestamps and completeness
alone. It was measured, so there is no reason to leave the denominator free, and
a denominator that can move after results are seen is exactly the failure this
forecloses.

### 6.4 The measurement, taken before any fit

Computed from timestamps and bar completeness. No model, no label, no outcome.

| quantity | value |
| --- | --- |
| spine rows | 45,802 |
| eligible rows | **44,171** (0.9644) |
| ineligible rows | 1,631, spanning rows `[0, 1630]`, contiguous at the head |
| binding constraint | the 78-bar warm-up of the **daily** series |
| first eligible timestamp | `2020-03-23T00:00:00+00:00` |
| inner-block eligible fraction, folds 0–3 | 1.000, 1.000, 1.000, 1.000 |
| outer-block eligible fraction, folds 0–3 | 1.000, 1.000, 1.000, 1.000 |
| training-block eligible fraction, folds 0–3 | 0.9248, 0.9385, 0.9480, 0.9549 |
| **folds available** | **4 of 4** |

Every ineligible row lies in the training block of every fold; no inner or outer
block loses a single row. **All four folds are available and the deciding bar is
3 of 4.** That is settled here, before the hypothesis is tested, and it cannot
move afterwards.

## 7. Target, horizon and costs — unchanged

`TargetSpec(horizon=6, fee_rate=0.0005, slippage_rate=0.0005)`, cost threshold
0.002, round-trip cost 0.002 charged as a flat subtraction from each trade's
return. Identical to v4, P2a, P2b, P2c, P3 and P4.

**The horizon does not move**, and the reason is the one
[`p4_preregistration.md`](p4_preregistration.md) §7.1 wrote down: the control's
entire evidence base exists at 1h/6h and nowhere else, so moving the horizon
replaces the control with an unmeasured object and changes two things at once.
After five checkpoints, changing the horizon without a scientific reason is
horizon shopping. A horizon checkpoint is a **different** checkpoint with its own
control and its own preregistration.

## 8. Validation design and the decision rule

### 8.1 Folds

The existing four temporal outer folds, unchanged, from
`nn.p2b.plan_from_manifest` over the committed manifest:

    fold 0  train [0, 21697)   inner [21697, 26518)  outer [26518, 31339)
    fold 1  train [0, 26518)   inner [26518, 31339)  outer [31339, 36160)
    fold 2  train [0, 31339)   inner [31339, 36160)  outer [36160, 40981)
    fold 3  train [0, 36160)   inner [36160, 40981)  outer [40981, 45802)

No shuffle. **The blocks are not split into more folds**: fold `k`'s inner block
is fold `k−1`'s outer block, the effective number of independent temporal
observations is already fewer than four, and subdividing them would manufacture
independence rather than find it.

### 8.2 The deciding cell

**`xgboost × ohlcv14_plus_mtf_v1`** against **`xgboost × ohlcv14`**, at cost
multiplier 1.0.

Logistic regression and LightGBM are run over all three arms and reported in full
whatever they show. They are **context only** and cannot switch the deciding cell
after the fact: a checkpoint whose winner is chosen from three models after
seeing all three has three chances to pass a bar written for one.

### 8.3 The rule

**Primary statistic:** the number of outer folds in which the deciding delta —
`ohlcv14_plus_mtf_v1` minus `ohlcv14`, outer-validation cost-aware net return,
same fold index — is **strictly greater than zero**.

**Improved means `delta > 0`, strictly.** A delta of exactly zero is not an
improvement: the claim under test is that the new information adds something, and
adding nothing is the null rather than a win. Zero is reachable in practice — two
arms that take no trades in a fold both return exactly 0.0 — which is why it is
settled here instead of when it happens.

**Bar: at least 3 of 4 folds improved.**

That is P2b's, P2c's and P3's rule unchanged. P5 asks P2b's question about a
different family, and an information-set checkpoint that scored itself
differently from the four it is compared against would not be comparable to them.

**Mean delta and worst-fold delta are descriptive.** They are reported for every
arm and every model. They may **not** rescue a fold-count failure, and they may
**not** veto a fold-count pass. P2b had two arms with a positive mean while
improving one and two of four folds; the count-the-folds rule was predeclared for
exactly that, and it is not weakened here by being given a second opinion.

**No further success criterion may be added.** §12 lists what may not change
after a result exists, and "one more condition" is on it.

### 8.4 Trade counts — reported, never decisive

Per-fold outer trade counts are reported for both arms. A fold in which either
arm realises fewer than **10** non-overlapping outer trades is **flagged**, and
the flag limits how far the result generalises.

It does **not** change the denominator. P4 excluded such folds; P2b, P2c and P3
did not. P5 follows the information-set precedent and reports the count, so that
the difference between the two conventions is visible instead of silent.
Excluding a fold changes the denominator, and a denominator that can move is a
denominator someone can move after seeing the numbers.

### 8.5 The false-positive rate, stated rather than hidden

Under an independent `p = 0.5` null, **P(X ≥ 3 of 4) = 5/16 = 0.3125**. A coin
clears this bar almost a third of the time, and the four folds are not
independent. P4's §8.1 established that and it does not stop being true here.

P5 does not pretend otherwise. It reports an exploratory answer with its error
rate written down, which is precisely why a positive P5 is a **lead** and not a
finding, and why §9 forbids treating one as confirmation.

## 9. What each outcome means

### 9.1 At least 3 of 4 improved

P5 is **supportive exploratory adaptive evidence**. It is not confirmation.

- It does **not** open Styx.
- It does **not** license a live allocation.
- It does **not** become a finding by being written up as one.

A positive result on eight-times-read blocks needs confirmation these blocks
structurally cannot supply. The correct next step is to design a checkpoint that
could supply it — not to act on this one.

### 9.2 Fewer than 3 of 4 improved

P5 is **negative**.

**Do not tune `mtf_v1`'s constants.** They were predeclared, and searching them
against these four outer blocks would convert a negative result into a fitted
one. **Do not create `mtf_v2`.** Five families on one asset at one horizon have
now failed; the next research move changes axis rather than appending a sixth
handcrafted family.

### 9.3 The availability gate fails

P5 is **not evaluable**: reported invalid/unanswered. An experiment that could
not run is not a negative result and is not written up as one.

### 9.4 Styx, in one sentence

The sealed region at `2025-08-27T23:00:00+00:00` is not opened, not read, not
planned over and not available to rescue an ambiguous P5 — and P5 adds no new
source, so it adds no new way to reach it.

### 9.5 P4-HOLD, in one sentence

P4-HOLD, rows `[45802, 48211)`, was retired unread by P4 with
`checkpoint: null`; the ledger in `nn.p4_holdout` is one-way with no path back to
`unspent`, and P5 does not ask for it, does not read it, and could not spend it
if it did.

## 11. Artifact and evidence policy

| class | where | immutable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **primary** — nine cells, `p2b.json`, `p2b.md`, `STATUS.md`, `outer_predictions.parquet` | `artifacts/benchmark/btc_p5_{arm}_{model}/` | yes — `artifacts/btc_p5_SHA256SUMS.txt` |
| **decision** — the mechanical application of §8.3 | `artifacts/benchmark/btc_p5_decision/` | yes — `artifacts/btc_p5_decision_SHA256SUMS.txt` |
| **derived** — the comparison | `artifacts/benchmark/btc_p5_comparison/` | no; regenerated and pinned by checking what it says |

The decision record is frozen because it is *what the checkpoint answered*. P4
froze its Stage-1 screen for the same reason. The comparison is not, because it is
regenerated whenever the aggregator improves and a hash over it would be a promise
this workflow breaks on purpose.

## 12. Forbidden after any valid P5 result exists

Once the first valid P5 outer number exists, none of the following may change.
An amendment is legal **only before the first fit** and **only as its own
commit**, with its reason recorded.

- the question or the hypothesis;
- the `mtf_v1` feature definition — timeframes, bar grid, completeness rule,
  feature engine, warm-up, alignment rule, contiguity policy;
- the arms or their names;
- the model families or their hyperparameters;
- the deciding model;
- the deciding comparison;
- the target, the horizon or the cost model;
- the fold plan or the number of folds;
- the eligibility conditions or the availability rule;
- the decision rule, the improved rule or the required fold count;
- **the addition of any further success criterion**;
- re-running a valid cell because its number is disappointing.

A genuine software or runtime failure may be fixed and the affected **invalid**
cell re-run. Invalid execution and disappointing valid evidence are different
things, and the difference is whether the cell produced a number under the
preregistered design at all.

### Errata

One field in the frozen design above is wrong, and it stays wrong.
`MEASURED_AVAILABILITY["eligible_fraction"]` is `0.964368`, which is 44,170 of
45,802; the counts beside it are 44,171 of 45,802, which is `0.9643902…` and
rounds to `0.96439`. It is an off-by-one, in a field derived from two counts that
are themselves right.

It is not corrected, because the field is in the hashed payload: changing it moves
the preregistration hash, and every one of the nine cells records the current one.
A preregistration that edits itself after the result is in is no longer a
preregistration, and the cost of holding that line is occasionally carrying a
visible wrong digit rather than a corrected document nobody can check against the
cells.

**The counts are the authoritative figures** — 44,171 eligible, 45,802 spine rows,
1,631 ineligible. They are what §6.4 states, what every P5 cell recomputes from
the snapshot and reports as its own `eligible_fraction` of `0.96439`, and what
`tests/test_p5_preregistration.py` asserts; the fraction in the constant is
asserted by no test and read by no code. Nothing in the decision depended on it:
the availability rule of §6.2 is a per-block fraction and the gate of §6.3 is a
fold count, both recomputed at run time, and §8.3 counts folds. No number in this
closure is derived from the eligible fraction.
