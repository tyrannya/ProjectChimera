## 1. The question, and why this axis

> Can independent native-timeframe specialists extract robust cost-aware
> predictive signal at temporal scales the previous 1h/6h programme did not test?

Five information families have failed: `smc_v1`, `chart_structure_v1`,
`microstructure_v1`, `derivatives_v1`, `mtf_v1`. Every one of them was measured on
**one clock** — a 1h bar with a six-bar label — and the clock is the axis none of
them varied.

`P5` looks like an exception and is not. `mtf_v1` evaluated the same fourteen
features over fully closed 4h and 1d bars and **aligned them onto a 1h row**. The
decision was still taken hourly, by a model fitted on hourly samples, scored
against an hourly control. No checkpoint in this repository has ever fitted a
model on 1m, 5m, 15m or 30m samples.

That is also the architecture this system was designed around — independent
temporal specialists whose agreement Pythia would fuse — and it has never been
tested. P6 tests the specialists. `P7` tests the agreement, and only after P6 is
closed.

**No hypothesis is asserted.** Five negatives on one clock make the clock worth
testing and say nothing about which way it comes out.

## 2. What P6 changes, and what it holds

| held fixed | varies |
| --- | --- |
| asset, exchange, instrument | the clock |
| the fourteen `chimera.features` columns | the physical horizon (six *native* bars) |
| label definition and cost threshold | — |

| seed, `seq_len`, threshold selector and grid | — |

`smc_v1`, `chart_structure_v1`, `microstructure_v1`, `derivatives_v1` and
`mtf_v1` are **absent**. A checkpoint that changed the clock *and* the

## 3. Source

`data/research/btc_usdt_multiclock_gen2_1m_pre_boundary.parquet` and specified in
[`multiclock_v1.md`](multiclock_v1.md). Bars are cut on the epoch-anchored UTC

The derived 1h clock agrees with the committed 1h research history on **47,094 of

## 4. The clocks, and the horizon rule

Five clocks, frozen. Not four, not six, and not "whichever of these five
survives".

| clock | horizon (6 native bars) | region rows | segments | HOLD share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `1m` | 6 minutes | 2,821,619 | 17 | 0.805 |
| `5m` | 30 minutes | 563,328 | 17 | 0.544 |
| `15m` | 90 minutes | 186,936 | 16 | 0.367 |
| `30m` | 3 hours | 92,834 | 16 | 0.278 |
| `1h` | 6 hours | 45,783 | 15 | 0.203 |

**Six native bars is the scale-consistent rule.** The number six is inherited
from every previous checkpoint; the *unit* is what changes. Keeping "six bars"
fixed and letting the physical distance follow the clock is what makes the five
cells one experiment rather than five unrelated ones. Searching horizons is a
different checkpoint — `P9` — and §10 forbids it here.

**The class balance is an output, not an input.** A fixed 20 bps round-trip cost
threshold is a large fraction of a six-minute move and a small fraction of a
six-hour one, so HOLD runs from 80.5% of 1m rows to 20.3% of 1h rows. This is a
mechanical consequence of holding costs fixed while the clock changes. The table
is recorded here, before any fit, precisely so that nobody can later claim the
labels were chosen once the balance was known. Moving the threshold, the class
definition or the horizon in response to it is forbidden.

The 1h clock's 45,783 rows against the committed snapshot's 45,802 is the 13
hours the full-constituent rule makes unavailable, plus the segment boundaries
they create. **P6's 1h cell is not a reproduction of P2a's control and is not
offered as one.**

## 5. Samples

`seq_len = 1`: one native bar of the fourteen columns, identical on every clock,
so no clock is advantaged.

**Why not 64.** P2a and P2b window 64 bars because they scored MTST's own samples
and had to build the array MTST built. P6 has no MTST and no such constraint, and
"native OHLCV14 semantics on this clock" reads most exactly as *the fourteen
columns of the bar the decision is taken on*. The fourteen are already multi-bar
aggregates — EMA ratios, RSI-14, MACD, ATR-14, realised volatility, a volume
z-score — so history reaches the model through the indicators rather than through
896 flattened columns.

A second, weaker reason is recorded rather than hidden: at 1m the fold-3 training
block is 2.24 million samples, and at `seq_len = 16` a single logistic-regression
fit on it reached **9.0 GB** of resident memory on a 15 GB machine before either
tree family ran; at 64 the input array alone is 8 GB. That measurement was taken
on a *training* block, before any outer block existed in any form. It ruled out
the alternative; it did not choose the value.

## 6. Folds — the same four real-world periods on every clock

Mapped by **timestamp**, never by row number. A 1m row 21,697 and a 1h row 21,697
are two different fortnights.

The instants are `nn.p2b.plan_from_manifest`'s output over the committed 1h
snapshot — the planner every checkpoint since P2b has run — rendered from row
indices into times. `tests/test_p6_preregistration.py` recomputes them from that
snapshot and asserts they are exactly these, so the freeze is checkable rather
than asserted.

| fold | train | inner validation | outer validation (reported) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 2020-01-04T06:00Z → 2022-08-15T10:00Z | → 2023-03-04T07:00Z | → 2023-09-24T17:00Z |
| 1 | 2020-01-04T06:00Z → 2023-03-04T07:00Z | → 2023-09-24T17:00Z | → 2024-04-12T14:00Z |
| 2 | 2020-01-04T06:00Z → 2023-09-24T17:00Z | → 2024-04-12T14:00Z | → 2024-10-30T11:00Z |
| 3 | 2020-01-04T06:00Z → 2024-04-12T14:00Z | → 2024-10-30T11:00Z | → 2025-05-19T08:00Z |

All blocks are half-open on bar-open time. The last outer block ends exactly at
the research-visible boundary.

**Four periods, whatever the row count.** A faster clock delivers more rows inside
the same four windows. Rows are not folds, and 2.8 million minutes of one asset
over four windows is four observations of a market regime. The periods are not
subdivided to manufacture independence — the roadmap forbids it and so does this.

The six-bar label embargo is applied by `nn.dataset.sample_indices`, so no
training or inner row's label can be drawn from the block it is selected or
scored on, and no window or feature crosses a market-data gap.

## 7. Models

The three predeclared families from `nn.simple_models` at the configurations P2a
froze: `logistic_regression`, `lightgbm`, `xgboost`. **No hyperparameter search,
no early stopping, no per-clock tuning.** Seed 42, per-fold seed `42 + fold`,
exactly as `nn.benchmark` derives it. One run: logistic regression takes no seed
and the tree families are deterministic given theirs, so a second seed would not
be a second observation of these four periods.

**XGBoost is the deciding family**, chosen before any P6 fit for the reason P2a
established — it was the strongest OHLCV14 family there. The other two are
reported for every clock and decide nothing. If logistic regression passes the
gate on a clock where XGBoost does not, **the clock's verdict is XGBoost's**.

Thresholds are chosen by `nn.evaluate.select_threshold` on the **inner** block —
grid `arange(0.34, 0.91, 0.02)`, `min_trades = 10`, objective "maximise net
return after round-trip costs". No outer block reaches threshold selection.

## 8. Costs

Unchanged and in basis points: `fee_rate = 0.0005`, `slippage_rate = 0.0005`,
round-trip `cost_threshold = 0.002`. Applied once per realised trade by
`nn.evaluate.realised_trades`, which is the single definition of what trades a
signal series took and what each netted.

**A cost model expressed per trade does not become cheaper because trades are
shorter.** That is precisely the effect P6 measures, and discounting it would
delete the result rather than produce one. Turnover (`2 × trades`) and trade count
are reported per cell so a reader can see what the cost model is doing.

## 9. The verdict

### 9.1 P6 is a screen, not a comparison

There is no control arm, because there is nothing to be incrementally better
than: a 1m specialist has no 1m predecessor. Every existing gate in this
repository — P2b, P2c, P3, P5 and P4's stage-1 screen — compares an arm against a
control. None applies. So an absolute gate is written here, **before the first
fit**.

### 9.2 The viability gate

A clock's specialist is **viable on adaptive research folds** only if **all three**
hold for its XGBoost cell:

1. cost-aware outer net return `> 0` in at least **3 of the 4** folds;
2. **mean** outer cost-aware net return across the four folds `> 0`;
3. beats the matching **native-timeframe momentum baseline**'s outer net return in
   at least **3 of the 4** folds.

Failing any one of them is failing. Buy-and-hold is a **reference, not a required
competitor**: it is a full-exposure long position over four periods that all rose.

The momentum baseline is the native one — `nn.benchmark.fit_baselines` resolves
`ema_cross` by name in the clock's own feature set, so a 1m specialist is measured
against a 1m momentum rule and never against an hourly one.

### 9.3 Five verdicts, all reported

P6 reports **five separate specialist verdicts**. There is no summary row and no
best-clock line.

- A clock that fails is published as failing. Hiding it is forbidden.
- Choosing the best clock and calling P6 positive is forbidden.
- Refitting a clock that failed is forbidden, under any pretext.

### 9.4 What each outcome licenses

- **A clock passes.** It is viable on adaptive research folds and is carried into
  P7 as a frozen specialist. It is not a deployable strategy, and its number is
  not out-of-sample evidence.
- **A clock fails.** Recorded as failing. It is still carried into P7 if P7's
  design names it: P7 asks whether agreement adds value over the *individual*
  specialists, and that question's answer must not depend on which specialists
  happened to clear an absolute floor.
- **No clock passes.** P6 is negative — changing the clock, on this asset, over
  these four periods, under this information set and these costs, did not produce
  robust cost-aware signal. P7 may still run: consensus among unprofitable
  specialists is a separate question, and a negative P7 on top is a cleaner answer
  than not asking.

### 9.5 Evidence ceiling

**Exploratory, adaptive.** These are the same four real-world windows v4, P2a,
P2b, P2c, P3, P4 and P5 have already read, mapped onto faster clocks. No P6
result is confirmatory. A positive P6 would need confirmation these blocks cannot
supply; a negative one needs no discounting, which is the asymmetry that makes
negative results the cheap ones to trust.

### 9.6 Styx and P4-HOLD, in one sentence each

**Styx** (`2025-08-27T23:00:00+00:00`) is not read, scored, inspected or planned
over; the research-visible boundary binds three months earlier, so no P6 code path
reaches within three months of it.

**P4-HOLD** — rows `[45802, 48211)`, from `2025-05-19T08:00:00+00:00` — is retired
with `checkpoint: null`, was never opened, and is available to nobody; every clock
stops before it and P6 manufactures no replacement holdout because the clock
changed.

## 10. Forbidden once any valid P6 result exists

- changing the clock set, or dropping a clock whose verdict disappoints;
- changing the horizon rule from six native bars;
- changing the cost model, the fee rate or the slippage rate;
- changing the class definition or the cost threshold;
- changing `seq_len`, the seed, or any model configuration;
- changing the fold periods, or subdividing them;
- changing any condition of the viability gate, or the model that decides it;
- reporting the best clock as though it were the checkpoint's answer;
- refitting a clock that failed, under any pretext;
- adding a fourth model family, or a second seed, after seeing a number.

An amendment is legal **only before the first fit** and **only as its own commit**,
with its reason recorded and the superseded hash kept.

## 11. Causality — the leakage battery

Each item carries a positive control: a test that deliberately introduces the leak
and asserts the check catches it. **A check that has never failed is not
evidence.**

| id | property | positive control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | no bar is built from a minute at or after the research boundary | a minute moved to the boundary is refused by `resample_from_minutes` |
| L2 | no derived bar contains a constituent from the following period | a bar shifted one period is caught by the parity comparison |
| L3 | an incomplete bar does not exist rather than existing shortened | deleting one minute removes its whole bar, asserted by count |
| L4 | features at row `t` depend only on rows `<= t` on the same clock | `chimera.features`' append-invariance tests, per clock |
| L5 | no training or inner row's label is drawn from a later block | `sample_indices`' horizon embargo, asserted against the block edges |
| L6 | no feature, label or window crosses a market-data gap | segment ids recomputed per clock and asserted against the folds |
| L7 | the threshold is chosen on the inner block and never on the outer | the selector is handed inner indices only; asserted per cell |
| L8 | every clock's four outer periods are the same four instants | recomputed from the committed 1h snapshot and compared |

### Five things about this result matter more than the numbers

**The mean would have lied, for the third time in this programme.** Three of the
five clocks — `1m`, `5m` and `15m` — have a *positive* mean outer net return
while improving only two of four folds. Pooling four periods would have reported
them as gains. The count-the-folds condition was predeclared for exactly this,
in P2b, and it has now fired on real data at four different temporal scales.

**Condition 3 was nearly free, and saying so is part of the result.** Every clock
beat its native momentum baseline in all four folds — but on the fast clocks
that baseline returns about `-1.000`. `MomentumBaseline` takes a position on the
sign of `ema_cross` at every bar it is allowed to, and at a 6-minute horizon with
a 20 bps round trip it pays the cost model roughly a hundred times per fold. On
these clocks "beats momentum" therefore means "does not trade itself to death",
which is a real floor and a very low one. It should not be read as evidence of
skill, and the gate did not let it be: condition 1 is what bound.

**Buy-and-hold beat the deciding specialist in 19 of the 20 clock-folds**, at
full exposure. On the `1m` clock it returned `+0.186`, `+1.628`, `+0.034` and
`+0.425` across the four periods; the other clocks see the same four windows to
within one bar and differ only in the last decimals. The exception is `15m` fold
2, where the XGBoost specialist returned `+0.094854` against buy-and-hold's
`+0.035290` — one fold, in the flattest of the four periods, on a clock the gate
still found not viable. Two more of the sixty cell-folds across all three
families beat it: `30m` LightGBM fold 2, and `1d` logistic regression fold 3 in
P6-EXT.

Buy-and-hold is a reference and not a competitor, exactly as it has been since
P2a, and neither beating it once nor losing to it nineteen times is a reason to
select anything.

### What this licenses, and what it does not

> Under this BTC/USDT design — the same fourteen causal OHLCV columns, a
> six-*native*-bar cost-aware label, a 20 bps round-trip cost model, three untuned
> model families and four adaptive temporal folds of one asset on one exchange —
> independent native-timeframe specialists on 1m, 5m, 15m, 30m and 1h did not
> produce robust cost-aware signal. Every clock reached exactly two of the four
> folds required.

It is **not** proof that fast clocks are uninformative, that minute data carries
no signal, or that a different horizon, information set, cost assumption or model
class would fail the same way. None of those was tested. It *is* evidence that
changing the clock alone, holding everything else at the values five previous
checkpoints used, does not rescue the programme.

**Do not respond to this by tuning anything.** The clock set, the six-native-bar
horizon, `seq_len`, the cost model, the class definition and the gate were all
predeclared, and searching any of them against these four outer blocks would
convert a negative result into a fitted one. §10 lists them.

### P7 still runs

P6 screened the specialists individually. P7 asks a different question — whether
causal *agreement* between independently trained specialists adds cost-aware
value over the corresponding individual specialists — and §9.4 predeclared that
the answer must not depend on which specialists happened to clear an absolute
floor. The frozen P6 XGBoost specialists and their frozen predictions are what
P7 replays. Nothing is refitted.
